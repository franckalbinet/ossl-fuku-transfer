Schedule

The fully fine-tuned and Fukushima-only models each get their own learning rate and number of epochs, chosen on the Fukushima training samples by 5-fold cross-validation. The test set plays no part. start gives each model’s starting weights: the pretrained model, or None for random weights. The learning rate finder, from each starting point:

Findings

The pretrained model comes from one pretraining run. The Fukushima-side training was repeated with five seeds. Training on MPS is not fully deterministic, and the variation between pretraining runs is not measured. All results use SNV followed by the first derivative.

Cross-validation on the training samples chose a maximum learning rate of 3e-3 for both the fully fine-tuned and the Fukushima-only model, the highest rate tried. It chose 40 epochs for the fully fine-tuned model and 80 for the Fukushima-only model, the longest run tried. Both choices for the Fukushima-only model are at the edge of the grid, so it may improve further. In cross-validation, the two models reached about the same validation R²: 0.75 for both, with standard deviations over the folds of 0.03 to 0.04.

With these schedules, the fully fine-tuned model is slightly better on the test set: an R² of log1p(CEC) of 0.74, a correlation of 0.86 and an RMSE of 2.8 cmolc/kg, against 0.71, 0.85 and 2.9 cmolc/kg for the Fukushima-only model. The standard deviations over the seeds are 0.004 and 0.014 in R². The standard deviation of the test CEC values is 5.6 cmolc/kg. Before tuning, with 21 epochs at 1e-3 for both models, the gap was much larger (0.72 against 0.59). Most of that gap came from undertraining the Fukushima-only model. The pretrained model needs half the epochs.

On OSSL validation samples within Fukushima’s CEC range (11.2 to 30.8 cmolc/kg), the pretrained model reaches an R² of 0.66 and a correlation of 0.86. Both tuned Fukushima models do about as well on the Fukushima test set.

The OSSL backbone does not work unchanged. Without training, it partly ranks the Fukushima samples (correlation 0.57) but predicts too low, with a bias of -3.0 cmolc/kg. A linear head on the frozen backbone gives an R² of 0.31, and fastai’s fine_tune, whose backbone trains at a maximum learning rate of 5e-6, gives 0.39. These models keep their default schedules.

Recomputing the BatchNorm statistics on the non-test Fukushima spectra (AdaBN) does not help. The zero-shot bias changes from -3.0 to +4.1 cmolc/kg, the R² stays near -0.65, and the correlation stays at 0.55. The failure to transfer lies in the features, not in the BatchNorm statistics.

The fully fine-tuned and Fukushima-only models have similar errors in each soil type, year and batch.
