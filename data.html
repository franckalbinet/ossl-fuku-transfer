Fukushima data

Load the Fukushima MIR spectra and soil properties as a Spectra, for the current sample selection 
from fastcore.test import *
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

The Fukushima data is not public. The cells that read it are marked #| fukudata, and nbdev-test skips them unless you run nbdev-test --flags fukudata. The outputs on this page come from a local run.

The soilspec workspace keeps the data in data/, beside the project folders. Set SOILSPEC_DATA to use a folder elsewhere. The Fukushima data is in its fukushima folder:

Sample selection

The selection is provisional. It can change after review with the Fukushima colleagues.

It starts from the 1,592 samples of the earlier Cs137 ratio study, listed in reference/cs137_ratio_metadata.csv. Results stay comparable with that study. 1,401 of them have Thermo OMNIC .spa files. The samples in excluded are left out while any of their scans is in doubt:

Properties shared with OSSL

Seven properties are measured in both datasets: exchangeable K, Mg and Ca, CEC, total C, total N and pH in water. Fukushima reports the exchangeable cations as mg of oxide per 100 g of soil. The conversion to cmolc/kg divides by the oxide’s mass for 1 cmolc/kg of the cation, from the molar masses of K₂O, MgO and CaO (94.2, 40.3 and 56.1 g/mol), halved for Mg and Ca, which carry two charges. Fukushima’s CEC, in meq per 100 g, equals cmolc/kg. C and N are taken to be in % in both datasets. The MgO and CaO factors and the units of C and N are still to confirm with the Fukushima data team.

fuku_shared_props and ossl_shared_props give these properties in the same units, with clay (below 2 µm in both), one row per sample. Fukushima’s silt and sand follow other size limits than OSSL’s, so they are left out.

Bruker spectra

377 of the samples were also scanned on a Bruker spectrometer: the 316 Kazu samples (IDs 2600 and up) and 61 others. The Bruker spectra are CSV exports of wavenumber and absorbance, one file per replicate, named such as 2600a.csv or 1624_1.csv. bruker_files lists them by sample ID:

load_bruker

def load_bruker(
    wns, # Wavenumbers to interpolate onto, in cm⁻¹, in increasing order
    ids:NoneType=None, # Sample IDs to load; defaults to every sample in `bruker_files()`
)->soilspecdata.types.Spectra: # One spectrum per sample, with the soil properties in `source`

Load the Bruker spectra, averaging the replicates of each sample

bruker_files

def bruker_files()->dict: # Sample ID: CSV files of its replicates

The CSV exports of the Bruker spectra of the Fukushima soil samples, by sample ID

The Bruker files cover 498 to 3997.5 cm⁻¹, every 2.05 cm⁻¹. They end just below the 3998 cm⁻¹ of shared_wns, so comparisons with Bruker spectra stop at 3996 cm⁻¹. All 377 samples also have Thermo .spa files:

bfiles = bruker_files()
paired = sorted(set(bfiles) & set(spa_files()), key=int)
br = load_bruker(np.arange(650, 3997, 2), paired[:3])
len(bfiles), len(paired), sum(int(i) >= 2600 for i in paired), br.spectra.shape

load_fuku

load_fuku(wns=array([ 650,  652,  654, ..., 3994, 3996, 3998], shape=(1675,)), ids=None) -> soilspecdata.types.Spectra

Load the Fukushima spectra, averaging the replicates of each sample

load_fuku interpolates each .spa file onto shared_wns, 2 cm⁻¹ from 650 to 3998 cm⁻¹, then averages the replicates of each sample. The rows follow sample_ids():

fuku = load_fuku()
test_eq(fuku.spectra.shape, (len(ids), len(shared_wns)))
test_eq(fuku.sample_ids, ids)
fuku.spectra.shape, fuku.measurement_type
((1389, 1675), 'abs')

load_replicates keeps each replicate as its own spectrum instead of averaging them. sample_ids repeats each sample’s ID once per replicate. Differences between the replicates of a sample come from the measurement, not from the soil:
